          Scientific Explanation of the Train CV vs. Test Shift: As documented in the official ESA post-competition paper (Uriot et al., 2022), 505 synthetic Monte Carlo events were injected into the dataset, yielding a ~2.3× to 3.0× higher high-risk positive prevalence in the test split than in the test-like training split. A higher positive base rate naturally reduces the false-positive-to-true-positive ratio at a given operating threshold (increasing F2 from 0.5236 to 0.7684), whereas hard-to-predict late-escalating high-risk test events increase MSEHR from 0.2713 to 0.4883, raising overall ESA Loss L = MSEHR / F2 from 0.5181 to 0.6355.
